Finding the Right Words

A Companion to the Finding the Right Words Field Guide

Created by David Feinstein, PhD

Finding the right words is a skill you need in the moment, with a client in front of you. The Practice Program recreates such moments in 144 clinical vignettes. Each gives you a client, a scene, and a choice about what to say next among several plausible options.

Each vignette joins a session already under way. You can assume the basics have been done: the client’s own charged words have been reflected back and tapped on where that was called for. So the question is never whether to honor the client’s language. It is where to go when you move beyond it.

Once you choose, you’ll see why each option works or doesn’t, and which of the language variations the best answer draws on. Each item ends with a Takeaway, the principle in a sentence or two, so you leave with a pattern you can use with your own clients.

The explanations of the wrong answers teach as much as the right one. Each shows why a reasonable response misfires at this particular point: a sound move at the wrong moment, a beginner’s overreach, or a phrase that sounds clinically astute but doesn’t move the session forward.

Based on Feinstein & Rajah (2026), Finding the Right Words, Frontiers in Psychology.
