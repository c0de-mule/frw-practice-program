Finding the Right Words · The Guide on a Page

The Compass and the Map

The compass shows where the next phrase should head. The map shows types of wording to get there. Three directions, seven variations each. Learn it well enough to leave it behind.

Attune

Meet and reflect. Establish safety, engagement, and resonance with what is present. At a SUD of 8 or higher, tap with few words or none.

  1. Naming the ProblemName it accurately. Being heard is itself therapeutic.
  2. Validating Reasons to Keep the ProblemAcknowledge why it’s hard to give up: “It’s no wonder that I . . .”
  3. Recognizing Conflicting FeelingsMake room for both. Each side gets its full say.
  4. Discerning Multiple LayersOne feeling lies beneath another. Name it as the client begins to sense it.
  5. Using the Client’s Exact WordsGive back the charged word, or the whole sentence, word for word.
  6. Softening Harsh AbsolutesStart with their exact words; soften once the charge comes down.
  7. Using Humor or IronyOnly when the alliance is solid and the client has shown some lightness.

Explore

Question and uncover. Trace the difficulty to its roots and the logic behind it.

  1. Connecting Past and PresentLink what is felt now to where it began: “What does this remind you of?”
  2. Examining Costs and BenefitsHold both sides together so the trade-off becomes visible.
  3. Uncovering Patterns and TimingName when it shows up. Seeing the logic eases its hold.
  4. Challenging Limiting BeliefsWhose assessment was it, and is it still true?
  5. Exploration via MetaphorUse the client’s own image, or invite one: “This problem is like what?”
  6. Investigating Secondary GainsWhat would be expected of them if the problem lifted?
  7. Inviting CuriosityWonder aloud rather than press for an answer.

Lead

Orient toward change. Point to resilience and resolution — often beyond recovery and into growth.

  1. Reinforcing Personal StrengthsName resources the client has shown or reported.
  2. Transcending Old BeliefsOffer a counter-example, or alternate the old belief with a freeing one.
  3. Re-experiencing Past Events SafelyOnce safety is established, revisit a memory anchored in present resources.
  4. Envisioning ChangeMake it plausible before it has happened. Keep it achievable.
  5. Using Bridging Statements“Not there yet, but something in me is starting to . . .”
  6. Consolidating GainsName what shifted so it takes hold.
  7. Strengthening Commitment to ActionOne concrete step, with the how and the resources.

Offered, not imposedA phrase works only if the client finds it fits. A nod, a sigh, or a softening usually confirms it.

When you reach past what was saidA phrase beyond what the client has revealed is a hypothesis. Check it: “True or false?” or “Is that right?”

A caution on leadingBefore the client feels met and the material has been explored, leading invites resistance. Collaborate on the wording.

Attune regularly. Explore when there is safety. Lead when there is readiness.
